# Demonstration data

**Synthetic data.** "Surat Diamond Works" is a fictional factory. Every capacity, rate, yield and cost
below was chosen to make a plausible, internally consistent example with a visible constraint at polishing.
None of it is an industry benchmark, and nothing here should be read as typical of a real manufacturer.

The data is defined in `backend/src/main/java/com/realitycheck/demo/DemoData.java` and created through the
same services the API uses, so it passes the same validation as user input.

## How it is created and reset

| Setting | Effect |
|---|---|
| `REALITYCHECK_DEMO_SEED=true` | At start-up, seed the factory if the database has no factory. Existing data is never touched. |
| `REALITYCHECK_DEMO_RESET_ENABLED=true` | Registers `POST /api/demo/reset` and the Settings screen's reset action. It **erases all data** and seeds again. |

Both are `true` in `.env.example` and `false` when unset. Turn both off for any deployment that holds data
you want to keep.

The seed is deterministic **for a given seeding instant**. Ids are derived from fixed keys, and every
timestamp is an offset from an anchor: 09:00 Asia/Kolkata on the Monday two weeks before the week in which
the seed runs. History is generated from the anchor up to the seeding instant and no further, so the
factory always has two to three weeks of past and work in progress "now". Seeding twice for the same
instant produces identical rows; an integration test compares a digest of every table. Resetting on a
later day produces a history that ends on that day.

The demonstration does not move on by itself. A week after seeding, the work in progress is a week stale
and more orders show as late; reset to bring it up to date.

One availability window is seeded: two of the six polishing wheels are out for the whole working day on
the Wednesday of the first week (09:00 to 18:00 local), reason "Scheduled wheel maintenance (synthetic)".
It appears in the history as a `RESOURCE_UNAVAILABLE` event.

## Workflow: Rough to polished

Rate is stones per hour **per processing slot**. Setup is per job. Yields are the fraction that leaves the
stage. Fixed cost is per job, in INR. "Slots" is how many jobs can run at once with every pool at full
capacity.

| # | Stage | Rate | Setup | Stone yield | Carat yield | Fixed cost | One slot needs | Slots |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rough Intake | 240 | 10 min | 100% | 100% | 50 | 1 intake clerk | 2 |
| 2 | Planning | 30 | 15 min | 100% | 100% | 120 | 1 planning scanner, 1 planner | 3 |
| 3 | Marking | 45 | 10 min | 100% | 100% | 60 | 1 laser marking unit, 1 marker | 2 |
| 4 | Cutting | 12 | 20 min | 98.5% | 62% | 200 | 1 laser saw, 1 cutter | 4 |
| 5 | Bruting | 18 | 15 min | 99.5% | 90% | 120 | 1 bruting lathe, 1 bruter | 3 |
| 6 | Polishing | 6 | 15 min | 99% | 82% | 150 | 1 polishing wheel, 2 polishers | 6 |
| 7 | Quality Inspection | 40 | 5 min | 97% | 100% | 80 | 1 grading microscope, 1 quality inspector | 3 |
| 8 | Dispatch | 300 | 10 min | 100% | 100% | 40 | 1 dispatch clerk | 2 |

Stage capacity in stones per hour is rate times slots: polishing manages 36, against 48 at cutting and 54 at
bruting. That gap is deliberate, so that later milestones have a constraint to find and scenarios such as
"add two polishing wheels" have something to change. Whether polishing is actually the bottleneck for a
given set of orders is a question for the simulation engine (Milestone 4), not something this table asserts.

## Orders and history

Orders are generated by `ProductionSeedSection`.

| | |
|---|---|
| Arrival | One order every three working hours from the anchor: `SDW-0001`, `SDW-0002`, ... |
| Batches | One per order; every third order has two |
| Batch size | 60 to 120 stones, at 1.10 to 1.50 rough carats per stone |
| Priority | 1 to 5, spread evenly |
| Due | 7 to 10 days after placing |
| Customer | "Demo buyer A" to "Demo buyer F" |

A seed run mid-week in the third week gives about 45 orders, 60 batches and 1,400 events.

The history is produced by a small first-come-first-served scheduler that respects the working calendar
and each stage's slot capacity, including the wheel maintenance, so the recorded past is one that could
have happened: an integration test checks that no pool is ever shown using more units than it has. Recorded
durations run between 8% faster and 15% slower than the configured rates, as real work does, and recorded
stone counts are whole stones. Most completed orders are dispatched two working hours later; every fourth
is left completed and waiting. One cutting completion is recorded a stone too high and corrected an hour
later, so that the history contains a correction.

This scheduler is **not** the simulation engine. It manufactures a plausible actual history; the engine
(Milestone 4) will predict from it. The two are separate on purpose, so that simulated results can be
compared with something the engine did not itself produce.

Orders arrive slightly faster than polishing can absorb, so a queue forms there and some orders run late.
That is the designed constraint mentioned above showing up in the recorded history.
